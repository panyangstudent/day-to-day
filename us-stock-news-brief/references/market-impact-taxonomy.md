# Market Impact Taxonomy

Use this reference when turning news into market analysis.

## Macro Channels

- **Rates**: Inflation, labor, Fed communication, Treasury supply, and growth surprises affect discount rates, bank net interest margins, housing, duration-heavy tech, and small caps.
- **Growth**: Consumer spending, industrial production, PMIs, earnings guidance, and credit stress affect revenue expectations and cyclicals.
- **Liquidity**: Fed balance sheet policy, Treasury General Account changes, repo stress, dollar funding, and risk appetite affect valuation multiples.
- **FX and commodities**: Dollar strength, oil, gas, copper, gold, and agricultural prices affect multinationals, energy, miners, transport, and consumer margins.
- **Policy and geopolitics**: Tariffs, sanctions, export controls, defense conflict, and elections affect supply chains, risk premiums, and sector winners/losers.

## Sector Mapping

- **Mega-cap tech / AI**: Watch cloud growth, capex, chip supply, export controls, data center power constraints, antitrust, and margin guidance.
- **Semiconductors**: Watch equipment restrictions, foundry utilization, memory pricing, AI accelerator demand, inventory cycles, and China exposure.
- **Banks**: Watch yield curve, credit losses, capital rules, deal activity, deposits, CRE exposure, and trading revenue.
- **Consumer discretionary**: Watch employment, wage growth, credit card delinquencies, retail sales, gasoline prices, and company traffic commentary.
- **Energy**: Watch crude inventories, OPEC+, geopolitical supply risk, refining margins, natural gas, and capex discipline.
- **Healthcare / biotech**: Watch FDA decisions, trial data, reimbursement rules, M&A, patent cliffs, and drug pricing policy.
- **Industrials**: Watch PMIs, order books, defense spending, airlines, freight, reshoring incentives, and input costs.
- **China ADRs**: Watch Chinese macro policy, platform regulation, US-China export controls, audit/delisting issues, RMB, and domestic consumption.

## News Item Analysis Pattern

For each high-impact item, answer:

1. What happened?
2. Why does it matter for earnings, rates, liquidity, regulation, positioning, or sentiment?
3. Which tickers, ETFs, or sectors are most directly exposed?
4. Is the market likely reacting to a new fact, a confirmation, or a rumor?
5. What data or price action would confirm or weaken the thesis?

## Confidence Labels

- **High**: Primary source or multiple reputable sources agree; market channel is direct.
- **Medium**: Source is credible but market channel has assumptions.
- **Low**: Early report, rumor, translation gap, or indirect second-order effect.
